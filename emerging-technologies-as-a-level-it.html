Artificial intelligence

  • AI enables computers to behave as a human would: reacting to input, making decisions, learning and solving problems.
  • Machine learning algorithms let an AI system develop its own knowledge from a knowledge base, learning what counts as a 'correct' or 'wrong' response.

Advantages

  • More precise and accurate than humans
  • Works 24/7, no rest needed
  • Analyses large data sets quickly

Disadvantages

  • Expensive and skilled to program
  • Large, costly storage needs
  • Raises ethical/moral issues; limited creativity

Autonomous transport systems

  • Self-driving cars use AI, GPS and sensors to navigate, avoid collisions and park without a human driver.
  • Autonomous trains use sensors to control speed, location, distance and doors — cheaper, but can't react to an unexpected problem such as a stuck door, and can't reassure passengers the way a human driver can.

Augmented reality

  • AR overlays computer-generated images (text, audio, animation, video) onto the real world using a camera and sensors, then a display merges the two.
  • Used in retail (seeing furniture/clothes at home), gaming (Pokémon GO) and surgery, where smart glasses can show a surgeon patient records, scans and vital signs without looking away.

Virtual reality

  • VR immerses a person in a fully computer-generated 3D reality, usually via a headset with a display and headphones; gloves or special shoes add extra input.
  • Used in gaming, industry (virtual meetings, surgical training), scientific research (modelling molecules) and tourism (visiting places virtually).

Robotics

  • A robot automatically performs tasks by combining microprocessors and sensors: sensors provide data, the microprocessor analyses it and sends output signals to control the robot.
  • Robots were introduced into industry by General Motors in the 1960s. They were used in factories to move the parts needed to assemble cars.

Advantages

  • Handles repetitive/dangerous tasks
  • Works non-stop, fewer errors
  • Can pair with AI to improve

Disadvantages

  • Can cause unemployment
  • No emotional response
  • Needs constant power and skilled maintenance

Holographic imaging

  • A hologram is a 3D image, usually created by splitting a laser beam off mirrors and merging the beams again.
  • Used for security (hard to forge — credit cards, currency, passports) and architecture (showing clients what a building will look like).

Holographic and 4th generation optical data storage

  • Laser beams are split and bounced off mirrors onto a crystal, storing a hologram; each crystal (about the size of a grain of sugar) can hold around 1 terabyte.
  • Very new and expensive; current holograms can only store around 300 gigabytes of usable data.

3D printing

  • A 3D printer builds a physical object from a CAD (computer aided design) model, printing many thin layers of material such as plastic, metal or concrete.
  • Used in dentistry, prosthetics, medical tools, housing, replacement parts, jewellery, and bio-printing of tissue and organs.

Advantages

  • Fast, consistent, cheaper per item
  • Flexible with complex designs

Disadvantages

  • Can be used to make counterfeit/dangerous items
  • Still limited to small items; power-hungry; plastic waste

  • Thermal vision detects infrared light (heat) given off by people, animals and fires.

Wearable computing

  • Worn on the body (smart watches, fitness trackers, glasses, headsets) to record data like heart rate, steps and blood pressure.
  • Used for employee reward schemes and patient monitoring, which can alert a professional or family member if there's a problem.

Blockchain

  • Each block stores a transaction plus a hashed image of the block before it, so blocks form a secure, hard-to-alter chain.
  • Widely used for cryptocurrency, but processing and storing transactions requires a lot of computing power and energy.

Internet of Things

  • The IoT connects everyday devices to the internet so they can share data with each other and automate tasks.
  • Can support independent living, e.g. automatic shopping orders, voice-activated doors, health alerts.

Advantages

  • Shares data across devices; automates tasks
  • Can help the environment through efficiency

Disadvantages

  • Data could be wrong or faked
  • Devices are expensive; hacked devices are a security risk

Molecular data storage

  • Stores data in molecules, as the body does in DNA, needing very cold temperatures (liquid nitrogen/helium, around −200°C).
  • Extremely dense: around 25,000 gigabytes could fit in about 2cm², but the cooling liquids are dangerous if spilled.
