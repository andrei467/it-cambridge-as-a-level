Impact of New and Emerging Technologies

New and emerging technologies affect individuals, organisations and the environment in ways that aren't always positive. This page looks at power consumption, e-waste, manufacturing and wireless energy, then works through six exam-style practice questions.

New and emerging technologies are having important impacts on many aspects of our lives. Earlier pages looked at advances in medicine, healthcare and scientific research. Here we consider environmental issues, and how new technology can affect individuals and organisations in different ways.

For example, a new technology may make a process more efficient for an organisation. However, this may mean that fewer employees are needed to work on the process, so people lose their jobs. Blockchain and cryptocurrency mean that the banking system is under threat from people who choose to use cryptocurrency rather than normal banking systems.

The environment

Emerging technologies have many positive effects on our lives. But they can cause issues for the environment.

Power consumption

Technology uses power. This may be batteries, rechargeable batteries or electricity. The more technology we use, the more power we need. The demand for electricity may increase the amount of pollution we create, because we often use fossil fuels to generate electricity. However, more countries use wind, solar and hydroelectric power. Ensuring we use renewable energy sources will help limit the impact we have on climate change.

E-waste and recycling

Another challenge is that the metals used in batteries and electronics are often thrown away. Batteries cause pollution if they are not disposed of properly or recycled. Discarded digital devices such as desktop computers, mobile phones and games consoles contain rare metals and plastics. These can cause huge levels of pollution if sent to landfill.

Many of the metals in electronics are not yet recycled. Metals such as lead and lithium cause pollution. Governments around the world are trying to encourage the recycling of technology, to limit the issue created by this kind of disposal.

However, emerging technologies can also be used to help tackle climate change. AI can be used to monitor current trends and patterns and predict future ones. This could identify ways to make processes more efficient. AI can help reduce pollution by creating better transport links or reducing traffic jams. It can also be used by industries such as farming, so that they can create the best growing conditions for crops.

Manufacturing processes

Emerging technology can benefit the environment by making manufacturing processes more efficient. This can reduce the materials used and the energy wasted. However, the demand for emerging technology in manufacturing means that we need more rare materials and expensive components to create the technology used.

Emerging technology also helps reduce the size of new devices and components. For example, microprocessor chips can be much smaller and more powerful because of developments in transistor technology. This means that manufacturing machines need to be updated to help make the new devices or components, which can create a lot of waste when a manufacturer has to upgrade every machine in its factory.

New technology often helps make manufacturing more automated. This means that jobs people used to do may be replaced by machines. However, machines still need humans to clean, maintain and operate them, so workers may need to retrain.

Energy from wireless signals

Much modern technology uses wireless signals to communicate. Portable technology uses battery power as a source of electricity. These two things are linked.

Wireless signals have energy. New devices are being developed that can convert Wi-Fi signals into energy. This energy can be used to charge batteries and power small devices — imagine being able to sit near a Wi-Fi source and have your mobile phone recharge automatically.

Energy from wireless signals could also be used to power very small electrical devices directly, such as medical sensors — for example a sensor that detects blood sugar levels in a diabetic person. This means that sensors and small devices in your house could be powered by having Wi-Fi signal points nearby, letting the sensors run all day long without needing to be plugged in.
